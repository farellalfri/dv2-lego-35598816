Built Brick by Brick

How to read

Each circle is a country. The bigger the circle, the more toys, games and sports goods it imported by value. Malaysia is shown in red, Australia in blue. Hover over a circle to see its exact value.

What this shows

The United States imported USD 54.2 billion, about 60 times Malaysia's USD 882 million. Even Australia, with a smaller population than Malaysia, imports about four times as much. LEGO designs and prices its sets for those big markets first, and Malaysian shoppers pay those prices converted into ringgit.

Note: the figures cover all toys, games and sports equipment, not only LEGO. Source: OEC, imports under chapter 95 of the Harmonized System (HS92), 2022.

How to read

Each row is a theme group and each column a year. Darker cells mean more sets were released. Drag across the grey strip underneath to zoom into a period, and double-click it to reset.

What this shows

LEGO released no licensed sets until 1999, when the first Star Wars sets appeared. Within two decades, licensed themes became the company's largest group, every year since 2015. Over the same period, historical themes faded: none appear between 2017 and 2024.

Note: Based on Rebrickable's catalogue of sets with at least one part, excluding gear, books and promotional items.

How to read

Each small chart is one decade. Taller bars mean a bigger share of that decade's sets had that many pieces. Each labelled step along the bottom is ten times bigger than the last.

What this shows

The typical set grew from 56 pieces in the 1970s to 177 in the 2020s, with most of the growth after 2000. Sets with over 1,000 pieces went from almost none to about one in nine.

Note: The 2020s cover 2020 to 2025. Each bar shows a share of the decade's sets, so decades with different numbers of sets can be compared.

How to read

Each bar shows the years a theme was on sale. Gaps mean the theme was paused, then brought back.

What this shows

Castle ran for almost four decades before disappearing in 2016, and Pirates came back in five separate waves. Most other historical themes lasted only a few years. Culturally inspired themes now dominate this space: Ninjago has released 626 sets since 2011, more than all these historical themes combined, joined by Monkie Kid, based on the Chinese classic Journey to the West, and LEGO's Chinese Traditional Festivals sets, both familiar to many Malaysian readers.

Note: A run counts as continuous if there is no more than one year without new sets. Runs of fewer than three sets, mostly promotional items, are left out.

How to read

What this shows

A typical technical set costs about RM 164 at launch, against RM 123 for licensed and modern-day sets and RM 41 for the 'Other' group. Licensed sets reach the highest prices of all, up to about RM 3,476, beyond what many Malaysian households spend in a month.

Note: Prices are US recommended prices when each set was launched, converted at one rate (USD 1 = RM 4.09) and not adjusted for inflation. Only sets with a recorded price are included (5,168 of 12,757).

How to read

Each small chart is one theme group. The line shows what share of that year's sets were recommended for ages 12 and over.

What this shows

For a decade, only about 3% of LEGO sets were aimed at ages 12 and over. From 2019 that share climbed, reaching 15.5% by 2022, led by technical sets (about 40%) and licensed sets. Sets for young children and modern-day themes stayed firmly aimed at younger builders.

Note: Only sets with a recommended age are included (5,787 of 12,757). Few historical sets since 2009 have one, so that panel is mostly empty.

How to read

Each shape is a district. Darker districts spend more per household each month, on average. Hover over a district to see its value.

What this shows

Households in Petaling spend about RM 8,210 a month on average, more than three times as much as in Kalabakan (RM 2,257). The highest-spending areas cluster around Kuala Lumpur, while the lowest are in rural Sabah.

Note: Perlis, Kuala Lumpur, Labuan and Putrajaya use state-level figures. Six districts created after the boundary map was drawn (Membakut, Gedong, Sebuyau, Siburan, Pantu and Lingga) appear within the older districts they were split from.
