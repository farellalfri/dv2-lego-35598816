Built Brick by Brick

How LEGO grew up, went Hollywood, and who in Malaysia can still afford to play

46%

In Sabah, one LEGO castle costs almost half of a household's monthly spending. In Putrajaya, it costs less than a fifth. How did a box of bricks get here?

By Farell Naufaldi Alfri, 15th October 2026

Part 1

From bricks to blockbusters

LEGO is a global toy, but it is designed for the world's richest markets. Over five decades, it has also changed what it makes, from simple building sets to film and TV franchises.

The United States imports about 60 times more toys than Malaysia. LEGO designs and prices its sets for big markets like these, and Malaysian shoppers pay those prices in ringgit.

Licensed sets did not exist until 1999. Today they are LEGO's largest theme group.

The sets got bigger too: the typical set has tripled in size since the 1970s, and one in nine now has over 1,000 pieces.

Part 2

The past fades

As franchises arrived, the castles and pirates that once defined LEGO quietly disappeared. Their place has been taken by themes drawn from Asian culture.

3%

of all LEGO sets are historical themes, against 16% for licensed franchises.

Ninjago alone has released 626 sets since 2011, more than every historical theme here combined. Monkie Kid, inspired by Journey to the West, and the Chinese Traditional Festivals sets bring themes many Malaysian readers will recognise.

Licences did more than change the themes. They changed who LEGO is for.

Part 3

Built for grown-ups

LEGO increasingly designs for teenagers and adults. Its sets are bigger, packed with characters, and priced to match.

Licensed sets extend to the left and all other sets to the right. Each side shows its own shares, so the two can be compared directly.

For a decade, only about 3% of new sets were aimed at ages 12 and over. From 2019, that share climbed to 15.5%.

Each line is the typical set in one theme group. Each axis runs from the lowest group at the bottom to the highest at the top.

The box holds the middle half of each group's sets. The black line is the median: half the sets cost less, half cost more.

All five of these flagship sets cost over RM 1,000. Lion Knights' Castle, LEGO's castle comeback, was designed for adults and costs about RM 1,636.

So what does RM 1,636 mean for a Malaysian family?

Part 4

Who gets to play?

Household spending varies widely across Malaysia, so the same box means very different things in different places.

Households around Kuala Lumpur spend more than three times as much each month as those in rural Sabah.

Step by step, LEGO followed Hollywood and grew up with its fans. Its sets became licensed, bigger and aimed at older builders, and its flagship sets now cost well over RM 1,000. A typical set, at around RM 123, is still within reach almost everywhere in Malaysia, about 3.5% of a month's spending even in Sabah. But the castles, starships and display models LEGO now builds for adults are a different story. In Putrajaya, Lion Knights' Castle is a treat. In Sabah, it is almost half a month's budget.

About the data

  • LEGO sets: Rebrickable (sets with at least one part, 1970 to 2025, excluding gear, books and promotional items) and the Maven Analytics LEGO Sets dataset (1970 to 2022). Ages, prices and minifigures come from Maven and cover only sets with a recorded value.
  • Prices are US launch prices, converted at USD 1 = RM 4.09 and not adjusted for inflation. Malaysian shelf prices may differ.
  • Theme groups are based on Brickset's groupings, simplified to seven. Lion Knights' Castle is grouped as Technical because it was designed as an adult model.
  • Toy imports: OEC, chapter 95 of the Harmonized System (HS92), 2022. The figures cover all toys, games and sports equipment, not only LEGO.
  • Household spending: Department of Statistics Malaysia, Household Income and Expenditure Survey 2024. Perlis, Kuala Lumpur, Labuan and Putrajaya use state-level figures. Six districts created after the boundary map was drawn (Membakut, Gedong, Sebuyau, Siburan, Pantu and Lingga) appear within the older districts they were split from.
  • Population: Department of Statistics Malaysia, 2024 estimates. Boundaries: Department of Statistics Malaysia and Natural Earth.
